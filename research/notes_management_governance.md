### 3.3 Performance Metrics Tied to Compensation
- **Annual Cash Incentive**: Based on a formulaic assessment of:
  - Net sales (revenue) performance vs. internal target
  - Operating income performance vs. internal target
  - The Compensation Committee retains negative discretion (can reduce, not increase)
- **Performance-based RSUs (75% of equity award for CEO)**:
  - Relative Total Shareholder Return (TSR) vs. companies in the S&P 500
  - 3-year performance period
  - Payout range: 0% to 200% of target
  - Modifier based on absolute TSR (negative absolute TSR caps payout at 100%)

### 3.4 Say-on-Pay Vote Results
| Year (Annual Meeting) | Approval Rate | Notes                                          |
|-----------------------|---------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| 2023 (Feb 2023)       | ~64%          | Lowest in Apple history; led to Cook comp redesign |
| 2024 (Feb 2024)       | ~88%          | Strong rebound after restructured comp package   |
| 2025 (Feb 2025)       | ~90%+         | [VERIFY exact figure from 8-K filing]            |

- The low 2023 vote prompted Apple to:
  - Reduce Cook's overall target comp by >40%
  - Shift from primarily time-based RSUs to 75% performance-based RSUs
  - Introduce relative TSR as the sole performance metric for equity awards
  - Eliminate the discretionary component that had allowed upward adjustment

## 5. Corporate Governance Practices

### 5.1 Share Structure
- **Single class of common stock**: Apple does NOT have dual-class or multi-class shares.
  All shareholders have equal voting rights (1 share = 1 vote).
- This is a governance strength -- no insider super-voting control.

### 5.2 Proxy Access
- Apple adopted proxy access in 2015:
  - Shareholders owning 3% or more of outstanding shares for at least 3 continuous years
    may nominate directors
  - May nominate up to 20% of the board (or at least 1 director)
  - Standard "3%/3-year/20%" proxy access terms

### 5.3 Board Elections
- **Annual elections**: All directors stand for election every year (no staggered/classified board)
- **Majority voting standard**: In uncontested elections, directors must receive more
  "for" votes than "against" votes. Any director who fails to receive a majority must
  tender their resignation for Board consideration.

### 5.4 Other Governance Provisions
- **No poison pill** (shareholder rights plan) in effect
- **No supermajority voting requirements** for charter/bylaw amendments
- **Chairman/CEO separation**: The roles of Board Chair (Levinson) and CEO (Cook) are separate
- **Lead Independent Director**: James A. Bell serves in this capacity
- **Stock ownership guidelines**: Executives required to hold significant stock positions
  (CEO: 10x base salary in stock)
- **Clawback policy**: Apple has a compensation clawback policy in compliance with
  SEC/Nasdaq rules, allowing recovery of incentive compensation in cases of financial
  restatement
- **Anti-hedging and anti-pledging**: Directors and officers prohibited from hedging or
  pledging Apple stock
- **Director overboarding policy**: Directors should not serve on more than 4 public
  company boards (5 for non-CEO directors)
- **Annual board and committee self-evaluations**: Conducted regularly
- **Shareholder right to call special meeting**: Shareholders holding 10% or more of
  outstanding shares may call a special meeting (Apple adopted this provision)

## 6. Assessment for Fundamentals Analysis

### Governance Strengths
- Single-class share structure with equal voting rights
- Strong board independence (87.5%)
- Separated Chairman/CEO roles with an independent chair
- Robust capital return program aligned with shareholder interests
- Responsive to shareholder feedback (CEO comp restructuring after low say-on-pay vote)
- Annual director elections with majority voting
- Proxy access rights for shareholders
- No poison pill or supermajority provisions

### Governance Risks / Watch Items
- **CEO succession**: Tim Cook is 65 and has been CEO for 14+ years. No public succession
  plan. Key-person risk remains, though the deep bench of SVPs mitigates this.
- **CFO transition**: Kevan Parekh is new to the CFO role (1 year in). Early earnings calls
  and guidance will be closely watched for any shift in financial communication style.
- **Regulatory/antitrust**: DOJ antitrust suit (filed March 2024) and EU DMA compliance
  create governance/legal overhead and potential structural remedies.
- **Shareholder proposal trends**: AI transparency and labor practice proposals gaining
  traction; may require enhanced disclosure in coming years.
- **Board refreshment**: Several long-tenured directors (Levinson since 2000, Gore since 2003,
  Jung since 2008) -- average board tenure is high. While experience is valuable,
  fresh perspectives may be needed, especially in AI and digital services.

### Overall Governance Quality Rating: STRONG
Apple's corporate governance practices are generally best-in-class for a mega-cap company.
The single-class structure, independent board, shareholder-responsive compensation practices,
and massive capital return program demonstrate strong alignment with shareholder interests.
